
1. HSA (Health Savings Account) — Rank: 100

• Superpower: Triple-tax shield. Contributions lower your current taxable income, investments grow tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are completely tax-free.
• Special Skills:
	• No expiration timer: There is no time limit on when you can reimburse yourself for past medical expenses, allowing your money to stay invested for decades.
	• Level 65 unlock: Once you turn 65, you can withdraw money for any reason without a penalty (you only pay regular income tax on non-medical spending).
	• Portability: The account belongs to you forever, even if you change jobs or health plans.
• Limitations:
	• You must be enrolled in an eligible High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) to add money, which means higher out-of-pocket medical costs up front.
	• Strict yearly contribution caps apply ($4,400 for individuals in 2026).
	• Spending on non-medical items before age 65 triggers income taxes plus a steep 20% penalty.

2. Roth 401k — Rank: 95

• Superpower: Tax-free retirement wealth. Your investments grow completely tax-free, and every dollar you pull out in retirement is 100% tax-free.
• Special Skills:
	• Free ally loot: Employers often match a portion of your contributions, giving you instant free money.
	• High capacity: You can contribute significantly more each year ($24,500 in 2026) than you can to an IRA.
	• No RMD (Required Minimum Distribution) penalties: Unlike traditional workplace plans, you are never forced to withdraw money at a certain age during your lifetime.
	• No wealth cap: Unlike a Roth IRA, there are no income limits that block high earners from contributing.
• Limitations:
	• You can only access this account if your employer offers it.
	• You must pay income taxes up front before the money goes in.
	• Investment choices are restricted to the specific menu of funds your employer selects.

3. Roth IRA — Rank: 90

• Superpower: Tax-free growth and tax-free retirement withdrawals on a solo account you control completely.
• Special Skills:
	• Emergency escape hatch: You can withdraw your original contributions (the exact money you put in) at any time, for any reason, with zero taxes or penalties.
	• Total freedom: You can open this at almost any brokerage and invest in nearly any stock, ETF, or index fund.
	• No forced withdrawals: Your money can stay invested and grow tax-free for your entire life.
• Limitations:
	• You must have earned income (from a job) to contribute.
	• Strict annual contribution limits apply ($7,500 per year in 2026).
	• High earners are blocked from contributing directly once their income passes a certain threshold.
	• Withdrawing investment earnings before age 59½ generally triggers taxes and a 10% penalty.

4. 529 Plan — Rank: 85

• Superpower: Tax-free growth and tax-free withdrawals at the federal level when used for qualified education expenses.
• Special Skills:
	• State tax buffs: Over 30 states offer state income tax deductions or credits just for putting money in.
	• Broad target range: Funds aren't just for four-year colleges; they cover trade schools, apprenticeships, student loan payments (up to $10,000), and K–12 tuition.
	• Shape-shifting: You can change the beneficiary to another family member anytime, or roll up to $35,000 of unused funds into the beneficiary's Roth IRA.
• Limitations:
	• Using the money for non-education expenses triggers income taxes plus a 10% penalty on the investment earnings.
	• Investment choices are limited to the specific portfolios offered by the state plan you choose, but brokerages Fidelity and Vanguard offer state plans with additional options.

5. Traditional 401k - Rank: 80
• Superpower: Immediate, powered tax shield. Contributions are taken directly from your paycheck before taxes, lowering your taxable income right now while your investments grow tax-deferred.
• Special Skills:
	• Free ally loot: Employers often match a percentage of your contributions, giving you free money.
	• High capacity: You can stash away much more each year ($24,500 as of 2026) than you can in an IRA.
	• No wealth cap: Unlike a Traditional IRA, high earners are never blocked from making pre-tax contributions, regardless of how much they make.
• Limitations:
	• You can only access this account if your employer offers it.
	• Delayed tax bill: Every dollar you withdraw in retirement—both your original contributions and your investment growth—is taxed as regular income.
	• Early exit penalty: Taking money out before age 59½ generally triggers income taxes plus a 10% penalty.
	• Forced loot drops: Once you reach age 73, the government forces you to take Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) every year.
	• Investment choices are restricted to the specific menu of funds your employer selects.

6. Traditional IRA — Rank: 75

• Superpower: Up-front tax shield. Contributions can be deducted from your current year's taxable income, lowering your tax bill right now.
• Special Skills:
	• Special quest exemptions: You can withdraw money early without the standard 10% penalty for specific milestones, such as buying your first home (up to $10,000), paying for higher education, or covering major medical hardships.
	• Universal access: Anyone with earned income can open and contribute to one, regardless of how much money they make.
• Limitations:
	• All withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.
	• Forced loot drops: Once you reach age 73, the government forces you to take Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) every year, whether you need the cash or not.
	• If you (or your spouse) have a retirement plan at work and earn above a certain amount, you lose the ability to deduct your contributions on your taxes.
• Note: There is a similar account type, the traditional 401k that has some amazing advantages, though only offered through employers:
	• employers match a percentage of your contributions
	• higher contribution limit ($24,500 as of 2026)
	• high earners are allowed to make pre-tax contributions

7. ABLE Account — Rank: 85

• Superpower: Benefit shield. Allows individuals with disabilities to save and invest large sums without losing eligibility for vital government benefits like Medicaid and SSI.
• Special Skills:
	• Roth-style growth: Investments grow tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified expenses are completely tax-free.
	• Broad spending power: Funds can be used at any age for almost anything that improves health, independence, or quality of life (housing, transportation, education, assistive tech, basic living).
	• High limits: Standard contributions go up to the annual gift tax exclusion ($20,000 in 2026), and employed account owners can contribute even more.
• Limitations:
	• Strict eligibility: You must have a qualifying disability that began before age 46 (expanded from age 26 in 2026).
	• SSI cap: If the account balance goes over $100,000, SSI monthly cash payments are suspended until the balance drops back below that line (though Medicaid continues).
	• Medicaid payback: Upon the account owner's death, the state can claim remaining funds to reimburse Medicaid costs paid on their behalf before heirs receive anything.
